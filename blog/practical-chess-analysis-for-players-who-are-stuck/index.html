Practical Chess Analysis for Players Who Are Stuck

Feeling stuck often leads players to add more puzzles, more openings and more videos at once. A better first move is to inspect where your own decisions are breaking down.

Published 10 October 2026

A plateau is a description, not a diagnosis

Two players with the same rating can need very different work. One reaches good positions and loses them in time pressure. Another remembers openings but misses simple threats. Rating bands provide context, not a guaranteed list of weaknesses.

Find where good positions start becoming difficult

Opening transition

Do you know moves but not the plan after theory ends? Review the first position where you felt unsure, not only the first move the engine disliked.

Tactical control

Separate missed patterns from calculation errors. If you never considered the tactic, train recognition. If you saw it but miscalculated the reply, train the calculation process.

Conversion and defence

When ahead, check whether you allow unnecessary counterplay. When worse, check whether you create practical problems or drift into passive moves.

Clock use

Look at what you spent time on. Long thinks are useful only when the position deserves them. Repeatedly using half your time before the first critical decision can make later mistakes predictable.

Illustrative diagnosis

Good openings, difficult middlegames

A player scores well from a familiar opening but stalls once the centre changes. Their next study block should not add another variation. It should compare several transition positions and practise choosing between a pawn break, a piece improvement and prophylaxis.

Change one part of the routine

Choose the clearest repeated decision and work on it for a short block. Keep playing enough games to create later evidence. If the issue does not recur, record that honestly rather than declaring it fixed.

What to do next

Review five recent games and identify the first difficult decision in each. Compare your notes with the guide to finding repeated mistakes and the product page for practical game analysis.

The CheckmateMore loop

  1. Analyse your games
  2. Understand decisions
  3. Find repeated patterns
  4. Practise key positions
  5. Review later evidence
