How to Build a Chess Training Plan From Your Own Games

A useful training plan does not need many activities. It needs a clear link between the games you played, the decisions that caused trouble and the practice you can repeat this week.

Published 10 October 2026

Step 1: review a small, recent sample

Start with games that still feel familiar. Replay each one without the engine, mark uncertain decisions, then use analysis to check the critical positions. Avoid collecting every small inaccuracy.

Step 2: group comparable decisions

Look for causes that repeat. Opening memory, tactical scanning, exchanges, pawn breaks, conversion and clock use are useful areas, but the final description should be specific to your games.

Step 3: choose one or two weekly objectives

Make each objective observable

“Improve calculation” is vague. “Before every forcing move, calculate the opponent's checks and captures” gives you a routine that can be seen in later positions.

Keep the week manageable

One important habit practised properly is better than seven themes skimmed once. Add an opening or endgame study item only when the reviewed games support it.

Step 4: practise positions from the games

Use personal puzzles for tactical moments. For strategic choices, return to the position and compare plans. Explain the purpose of the preferred move before checking the line.

Illustrative weekly routine

A focused programme

Monday: review two recent games. Tuesday and Thursday: solve five personal positions and explain the theme. Saturday: play, then note every eligible opportunity for the weekly objective. Sunday: review the evidence without changing the objective too quickly.

Step 5: review what happened in later games

Keep, adjust or retire the objective according to relevant evidence. Do not treat a win as proof that every decision improved, or a loss as proof that training failed.
